Shrimpyscoller

A tiny crustacean. A very loud claw. An extremely annoyed government.

Shrimpyscroller is a short arcade-style shooter built in Unity as my Foundation Year final project. Inspired by the sonic abilities of the pistol shrimp, the game puts a nature-themed spin on the traditional shoot-'em-up: instead of flying a fighter or wielding a gun, the player takes control of a shrimp whose sonic booms have attracted the attention of the government.

Project Overview

I developed Shrimpyscroller as a solo project, taking the game from its initial concept through to a completed playable experience. The project was an opportunity to build my first video game in Unity while developing my understanding of gameplay loops, level design and game scripting.

Design Approach

The project began with a simple design question: what would a shoot-'em-up look like if it was built around nature instead of conventional weapons?

This gave the game a clear identity while allowing me to work within the familiar structure of an arcade shooter. The contrast between the harmless-looking protagonist and the increasingly absurd response from the government became a central part of the game's humour.

Gameplay & Level Design

I designed the gameplay loop around fast, straightforward arcade action, with the player's sonic attack providing the core interaction. I also designed the level progression and encounters to build towards the game's final confrontation.

The game's short format allowed me to focus on making each section contribute to the overall escalation, culminating in the player taking on a submarine in the final sequence.

Development

Shrimpyscroller was my first video game project developed in Unity. I worked independently across the game's scripting, gameplay implementation, level design and content integration.

I also imported and integrated the game's art and audio assets, giving me experience bringing together different elements of a project into a cohesive playable build.

Reflection

The project also reinforced the value of giving a game a strong central idea. Starting with the unusual combination of a pistol shrimp and a shoot-'em-up gave the project a clear identity and helped guide both its mechanics and its humour.

This experience provided a foundation for my later projects, where I have continued to explore how mechanics, narrative and a game's central concept can reinforce one another.
